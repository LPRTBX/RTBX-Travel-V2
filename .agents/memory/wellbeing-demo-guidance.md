---
name: Wellbeing demonstration guidance
description: Owner-approved public wellbeing UX and privacy rules for role handovers.
---

Keep the public wellbeing walkthrough a clear guest-support story, not an engine test form: a relevant approved hotel image, one current role action, truthful handover guidance and a compact role-filtered response timeline. Detailed sources, history and scripted-demo explanation belong in accessible disclosures.

**Why:** The owner rejected duplicate next-action panels, premature disabled controls, large empty communication/history cards, tiny labels and replacement-plan language on initial load.

**How to apply:** Compute guidance from actual unmet checks and current authority, including consent and capacity. A “Continue as” control changes perspective only; it must never approve, release, acknowledge or deliver. Independent checks may be performed in a different order without inventing completion.

Minimum-necessary role filtering applies to handover suggestions, next-action text and counts as well as messages, facts, summaries and history. Private feedback must not change a shared workload view or reveal its status through navigation.

**Why:** The owner explicitly requested role-appropriate information, including filtered counts/history, while keeping the fictional perspectives public.

**How to apply:** After a role finishes its task, it can hand coordination back to the accountable manager without exposing private feedback. Keep repair, guest feedback and timed follow-up visibly separate from support delivery; pending feedback is not a failed outcome or proof of improved wellbeing.

The owner also approved a PUBLIC, fictional “Guest feels unsafe” safeguarding story beside “Practical guest support”, not a live safeguarding service or medical advice. Preserve both stories independently.

**Why:** The owner wanted a more pressing reported concern while explicitly requiring consent, limited recipients, immediate-danger procedures and no real personal data, messages or calls.

**How to apply:** Use only explicitly reported facts, never inferred danger or threat scores. Do not collect current/proposed guest locations or room numbers. Routine coordination needs appropriate checks, review and recipient acceptance; the simulated immediate-danger path must remain available before routine consent or approvals. A request is not responder acceptance, and delivered support or “feels safer” feedback never establishes safety. Shared-role guidance and controls must not change because of private feedback or a private emergency request.
