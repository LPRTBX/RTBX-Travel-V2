# A three-night guest interaction guide

This is a worked full-service journey, not an industry average. The guest arrives in the afternoon, stays three nights and departs after breakfast. No issue, complaint or safety event is required. Services can be reduced or removed for a limited-service property or a guest who spends the day off site.

An interaction is one meaningful, complete guest-facing service exchange. A greeting, order and payment within the same meal episode are one interaction. A staff task, status update or retry behind that exchange is not another guest interaction. Shared room-party exchanges count once; individual rows assume guests interact independently.

## Walk through the stay as the guest

| # | When | What I do / receive | Counting basis | What the operation needs to coordinate |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Before arrival | I confirm the booking or arrival arrangements in a meaningful exchange. | Once per room stay | Reservation and arrival information agree. |
| 2 | Before arrival | I discuss a relevant room or service preference. | Once per room stay | An accountable team receives and can fulfil the preference. |
| 3 | Arrival afternoon | I complete check-in, receive keys and essential orientation. | Once per room stay | Room readiness, reservation, identity and front-office handoff. Count the complete exchange once. |
| 4 | After settling in | I ask one practical question or request an extra item. | Once per room stay | The request reaches an owner and receives an appropriate response. No failure is assumed. |
| 5 | First service window | I agree housekeeping timing or the room's service preference. | Shared room exchange | Respect the preference and coordinate room access. An unseen room clean is not itself a guest interaction. |
| 6 | First evening | I use the hotel's dinner service. | Shared room-party exchange | Table/service availability, dietary preferences and fulfilment. Seating, order and payment are one episode. |
| 7 | First service window | I make a separate coffee or refreshment visit. | Individual guest exchange | Service availability and fulfilment; allocate to a shared row if guests order together. |
| 8 | First service window | I ask about a local activity, facility or practical assistance. | Individual guest exchange | Appropriate information, availability, permissions and any partner handoff. |
| 9 | Morning after night 1 | I use breakfast service. | Shared room-party exchange | Eligibility, service capacity and preferences. One episode. |
| 10 | Second service window | I coordinate that day's housekeeping. | Shared room exchange | Align room access and the guest's schedule. |
| 11 | Second evening | I use dinner service again. | Shared room-party exchange | The day's service context, not a repeated claim of new value. |
| 12 | Second service window | I make a separate refreshment visit. | Individual guest exchange | A distinct service episode. |
| 13 | Second service window | I use an activity or assistance exchange. | Individual guest exchange | Relevant service or partner coordination. Set the rate below one if it is not used daily. |
| 14 | Morning after night 2 | I use breakfast service. | Shared room-party exchange | Daily service coordination. |
| 15 | Third service window | I coordinate housekeeping timing. | Shared room exchange | Daily service preference and room access. |
| 16 | Third evening | I use dinner service. | Shared room-party exchange | Daily service coordination. |
| 17 | Third service window | I make a separate refreshment visit. | Individual guest exchange | A distinct service episode. |
| 18 | Third service window | I use an activity or assistance exchange. | Individual guest exchange | Daily assistance or partner coordination. |
| 19 | Departure morning | I use breakfast service after night 3. | Shared room-party exchange | Departure-day capacity and timing. This is the third breakfast, not a fourth day added to the nightly rate. |
| 20 | Departure | I complete checkout and confirm my account. | Once per room stay | Account accuracy, payment and departure coordination. One exchange. |
| 21 | After departure | I engage in a meaningful feedback exchange. | Once per room stay | Feedback reaches an owner and informs the next cycle. An unopened survey or automated send does not count. |

## Count this example

- Six lifecycle exchanges per room stay: confirmation, preference, check-in, settling-in request, checkout and feedback.
- Three shared daily exchanges per occupied room night: housekeeping coordination, breakfast and dinner.
- Two individual exchanges per guest night: a separate refreshment visit and activity/assistance.

| Guest / occupancy basis | Formula for a three-night room stay | Guest-facing exchanges |
|---|---|---:|
| One guest | 6 + (3 nights × 3 shared) + (1 guest × 3 nights × 2 individual) | 21 |
| 1.5 guests on average across stays | 6 + (3 × 3) + (1.5 × 3 × 2) | 24 |
| Two guests, individual exchanges occur independently | 6 + (3 × 3) + (2 × 3 × 2) | 27 |

The 1.5 figure is a portfolio average, not a literal half-person. If two guests share refreshment or assistance exchanges, reallocate those to a shared basis or lower their individual frequency; do not blindly double the count.

## Adjust for what could happen

Optional additions include luggage assistance, airport-transfer arrangements, a relevant upgrade discussion, laundry, a separate room-service order, late-checkout arrangements, an experience booking or a return-visit conversation. Include them only when they occur, without recounting exchanges already included above. A wifi fault, missed transfer, maintenance defect or welfare concern creates a different operational branch; do not force these into every normal stay.

A limited-service stay can remove dinner, activity assistance, daily housekeeping exchanges and post-stay feedback. A room clean, sensor reading or system message is not automatically a guest interaction. The operating platform can still coordinate staff, system and partner events behind the guest journey, but those need a separate event inventory.

## Bring this into the calculator

Monthly guest-facing activity = (room stays × lifecycle frequencies) + (occupied room nights × shared daily frequencies) + (guest nights × individual daily frequencies).

At one site with 120 rooms, 75% occupancy, 30 days, three-night stays and 1.5 guests per occupied room:

| Component | Calculation | Monthly exchanges |
|---|---|---:|
| Lifecycle | 900 room stays × 6 | 5,400 |
| Shared daily services | 2,700 occupied room nights × 3 | 8,100 |
| Individual daily services | 4,050 guest nights × 2 | 8,100 |
| Total for this worked pattern | 900 room stays × 24 | 21,600 |

These numbers describe a chosen pattern, not observed demand. The calculator now exposes each row and its editable frequency. A rate of 0.5 assumes one exchange for half of the relevant stays/nights; zero removes it. Pre/post-stay exchanges are allocated to a completed stay for planning, not assigned actual calendar dates.

## Connect interactions to the operating loop

For each recorded exchange, ask whether there is a decision, coordination need, opportunity, preventative intervention, issue or welfare need. One operational moment may involve several exchanges. Identify it once, assign its primary category and owner, apply the policy and authority, coordinate action, retain evidence and check the outcome. A useful interaction is not automatically incremental value.

For a towel request, the guest request, internal assignment and completion update might be three operational exchanges supporting one moment. In this guest-facing guide, the request/response service episode counts once; its internal events sit in a separate ledger. Do not add the whole operational chain to the guest total or multiply revenue by every exchange.

A pilot should replace frequencies with observed exchanges and unique moment records, measure existing service performance, then compare the changed outcome and effort. The calculator's financial moment assumptions remain separate until that evidence establishes a defensible relationship.
