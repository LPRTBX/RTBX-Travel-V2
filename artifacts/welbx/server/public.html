The JALDO coordination loop

Six steps. One accountable response.

AI supports interpretation and options; configured rules set boundaries and authorised people decide. Teams act, adapt when conditions change, record evidence and review what was learned.

  1. Signal + context

    Connect a request or operational signal with its source, timing, promises and relevant capacity.

  2. Interpret + prepare options

    AI links the context, names uncertainty and drafts choices. It does not choose, diagnose or infer distress.

  3. Apply rules + decide

    Configured rules define limits. An authorised person decides where approval is required; guests choose whether to accept.

  4. Coordinate + adapt

    Named people receive actions and due times. If facts change, the plan can be reviewed and checked again.

  5. Record evidence

    Delivery, feedback and resolution remain distinct. Missing evidence stays visibly unconfirmed.

  6. Review learning

    People review outcomes and proposed improvements before any limited test or change.

      Intended outcomes

      What should feel different on a hard shift.

      For guests

      • One honest update from someone who owns their issue.
      • Promises are kept, or openly changed with a new answer, not quietly dropped.
      • Estimates are called estimates until a room is inspected.

      For teams

      • Know their next action, with an owner and a time.
      • Handover context travels with the work.
      • Capacity is checked before anyone commits them.

      For the GM

      • Sees ownership, what is overdue and what needs a decision.
      • Approves exceptions with the evidence in front of them.
      • Sees what is still open, not a false all-clear.

      Owners see a controlled recovery, with evidence. Groups see where support is genuinely needed.

      Intended design on synthetic data, not measured results. Outcomes must be checked by people in a bounded pilot.

      Why coordination matters to hotel teams

      The judgement is already in your team. The gaps are between them.

      Front office, housekeeping, maintenance, F&B and guest relations each work well in their own system. Handover context, ownership and guest promises can slip between desks and shifts.

      • Owners
