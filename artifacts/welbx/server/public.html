Who it is for, and why

The judgement is already in your team. The gaps are between them.

Front office, housekeeping, maintenance, F&B and guest relations each work well in their own system. Things slip in the handover: a note in one tool, a call to another desk, a promise nobody is clearly holding.

  • Hotel and resort operators
  • Owners
  • General managers
  • Multi-property groups
  • Disconnected systemsPMS, housekeeping, work orders and messaging each hold part of the picture. No one view shows what was promised to whom.
  • HandoversShift changes and desk-to-desk calls lose context. The next person starts again, or does not start at all.
  • Unclear ownershipEveryone saw the issue. Nobody was named. The guest asks twice.
  • No closed loopWork is done but not confirmed, or confirmed but not told to the guest. Lessons stay in someone's head.

What is coordinated

Not another system of record. The thread between them.

  • Guest momentsEarly arrivals, requests, complaints, recovery gestures.
  • Room readinessClean, inspected, fixed, released, and who confirmed it.
  • Service backlogOpen requests and work orders that are ageing or blocked.
  • HandoversWhat the next shift must know and own.
  • PromisesAnything said to a guest, with an owner and a due time.
  • Across propertiesOptional: shared patterns and support between sites.
Your existing systems stay the systems of record.

Any integration with a PMS or operations tool is proposed. It would need your permissions, the vendor's terms and validation in a pilot before anything is relied on. There are no live PMS connections today.

How it works

Six steps, one accountable loop.

  1. Signal

    Something needs attention: a late room, an ageing request, a guest message.

  2. Context

    What is known is gathered: the guest, the room, what was promised, who is on shift.

  3. Human decision

    A named person chooses what happens. Suggestions are offered, never imposed.

  4. Coordinated action

    The right teams get a clear task, an owner and a time.

  5. Evidence

    Completion is recorded only when it is confirmed. Missing receipt stays visibly unconfirmed.

  6. Reviewed learning

    Patterns are reviewed by people. Nothing is changed or deployed automatically.

Approval is a decision, not a guarantee. If housekeeping has no capacity, approving a promise does not create it. The loop is designed to show that gap early so the team can choose again.

Practical examples

How coordination could work on a shift.

Room readiness · Fictional illustration

Six arrivals, four rooms not released

A coastal resort expects an early group. At 12:40 four of six rooms are still in service.

Signal
Arrivals due before rooms are inspected.
Context
Two rooms cleaning, one awaiting a maintenance fix, one ready but uninspected.
Decision
Front office lead prioritises the inspection and offers the group luggage hold and lunch seating.
Action
Housekeeping supervisor and maintenance each get one owned task with a time.
Evidence
Three rooms confirmed released. The fix is not yet confirmed, so it stays open.
Learning
Review notes a repeating pre-arrival maintenance pattern for the GM to consider.
Service recovery · Fictional illustration

A request asked for twice

A guest's extra-pillow and air-con requests have aged three hours across a shift change.

Decision
Duty manager approves a recovery gesture and a personal call.
Evidence
Work order closed; guest call logged. The gesture stays unconfirmed until delivery is recorded.
Multi-property · Fictional illustration

Two sites, one pattern

A regional group sees late room release at two city hotels on the same weekday.

Decision
Cluster ops lead asks both GMs to review rostering, without forcing a change.
Learning
Proposed adjustments are reviewed per property before any is adopted.

All examples are fictional and illustrative. They describe the intended design, not measured results.

Your people retain authority

Review a proposed change before testing it; a replay does not deploy it.
